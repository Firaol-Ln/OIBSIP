THE SCIENTIST

A Remarkable Scientific Legacy

Marie Curie was a Polish-born physicist and chemist whose research made important contributions to the study of radioactivity. Her determination and interest in science helped her become one of the best-known scientists in history.

HER STORY

About Marie Curie

Marie Curie was born Maria Skłodowska in Warsaw, Poland, in 1867. She became interested in learning and science while she was young. At that time, women in Poland had limited opportunities to attend university, so she later moved to Paris to continue her education.

In Paris, Marie studied physics and mathematics at the University of Paris. Life was not always easy for her, but she continued her studies and worked hard. She later met Pierre Curie, and they became both married partners and research partners.

Marie and Pierre studied the strange rays produced by uranium. Their experiments led them to discover two new elements, polonium and radium. Marie named polonium after Poland, her home country. Their research helped scientists learn much more about radioactive materials.

After Pierre died in 1906, Marie continued her scientific work. She took his teaching position at the Sorbonne and became the first woman to teach there. During World War I, she also helped doctors by supporting the use of mobile X-ray machines to examine injured soldiers.

MILESTONES

Key Moments

1867

Born in Warsaw

Marie Curie was born Maria Skłodowska in Warsaw, Poland, in a family that valued education.

1891

Moves to Paris

She moved to Paris to continue her education and study physics and mathematics at the University of Paris.

1895

Marries Pierre Curie

Marie married Pierre Curie, and the two began working together on scientific research.

1898

Discovery of Polonium and Radium

Marie and Pierre Curie announced the discovery of polonium and radium during their research into radioactive materials.

1903

Nobel Prize in Physics

Marie Curie shared the Nobel Prize in Physics with Pierre Curie and Henri Becquerel for their research into radiation.

1906

First Woman to Teach at the Sorbonne

After Pierre's death, Marie took over his teaching position and became the first woman to teach at the Sorbonne.

1911

Nobel Prize in Chemistry

Marie received her second Nobel Prize, this time in Chemistry, for her work involving radium and polonium.

1914–1918

X-rays During World War I

Marie helped bring mobile X-ray technology closer to wounded soldiers so doctors could examine their injuries.

HER LEGACY

Why She Still Matters

What I find most interesting about Marie Curie's story is that she continued her work even when many things made her career difficult. She became successful in a field where women had very few opportunities at the time.

Her discoveries had an important effect on science and medicine. Her work with radioactivity also influenced later research into the use of radiation in medicine.

Marie Curie's life is a good example of how curiosity and persistence can lead to important discoveries. Her achievements continue to make her an important figure in the history of science.
